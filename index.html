How to read this map

Shapes say what a place is

  • ★ creek swimming place
  • ◉ City pool
  • ⌂ park reference (not a water entry)
  • ○ creek context only (optional layer; not a swimming place)
  • ◆ USGS gauge (optional layer; measures its station, not a swimming hole)
  • ◆ red gauge = a fresh 0 cfs discharge reading at that station
  • ▣ City Hydromet gauge (optional layer; stage and discharge where reported)
  • ★ dashed ring = location approximate or derived
  • ▲ chip on the frame edge = a saved destination off screen, arrow at its true direction, miles straight-line from downtown

Colors say what the evidence supports right now

  • Green — a gauge reading falls in a documented flow band, or a City schedule has a swim window today (switch to Open now for the current hour)
  • Yellow — the documented rule calls the fresh reading borderline
  • Red — too dry or too high under the rule, or a City closure / no published swim window in the selected view
  • No color — no current rating. Open the place card for the missing evidence or expired check.

A zoomed-out pie counts nearby places of one kind: each slice is proportional to places currently red, yellow, green, or without a rating (ivory). The center is the number of places; the small symbol shows their kind. Tap for names and individual evidence. A red gauge means no measurable discharge at that station; it does not rate every swimming place nearby. Most USGS flow cues can remain dated planning cues for up to six hours. For Blunn Creek, a reading under 30 minutes old is current; an older green-band reading can remain a dated cue for at most two hours when the three-hour flow-history check passes and no six-hour station rain is reported. The card gives the observation age. City schedule colors expire at the next relevant boundary or two hours after the oldest required City page check. Green never means clean, safe, deep, or permitted. Park shading is City parkland, not swimming permission. Creek lines are the City’s channel lines, not proof of water. Official NWS notices, a heat/sun planning band, and each station's hydrologic context appear on the card only, never in the place marker color.

Where can I swim?

Each place card separates what is measured (a gauge reading with its time), what is inferred (a named rule applied to that reading), what the City schedules or closes, and what is simply unknown.

A place marker without red, yellow, or green has no current rating. Its card names the gap.

Swimming places by name

Keyboard and screen-reader alternative to the map. Colored badges appear only where a current rating exists; each card explains its evidence or gap.

USGS gauges · measured context Live readings and six-hour trends from every displayed station; place and gauge cards offer 24-hour, seven-day, and seasonal discharge context where records support it. A station alone is never a swim verdict.

City Hydromet · measured context Official City creek gauges report stage, discharge, or both. Stage is not swimming depth. Tap a station for its observation time and source.
