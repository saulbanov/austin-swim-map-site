How to read this map

Shapes say what a place is

  • ★ swimming hole: creek, river or spring
  • ◉ pool or public water park
  • ✱ splash pad (no swimming)
  • ≈ lake beach
  • ⌂ park reference, not a water entry
  • ★ dashed outline: location approximate or derived
  • ▲ chip on the frame edge: saved places 25 or more miles from downtown, pointing their true direction, with straight-line miles and how many of each kind

Colors say what the evidence supports right now

  • Green — a gauge reading falls in a documented flow band, or posted hours have the place open today (switch to Open now for the current hour)
  • Yellow — the documented rule calls the fresh reading borderline
  • Red — too dry or too high under the rule, or a closure or no posted opening in the selected view
  • No color — no current rating. Open the place card for the missing evidence or expired check.

A zoomed-out pie stands for nearby places of one kind: the number is how many, and the slices show how many are red, yellow, green or unrated. Green never means clean, safe, deep or permitted. How long each color lasts, and what a gauge can and cannot tell you.

Where can I swim?

Each place card separates what is measured (a gauge reading with its time), what is inferred (a named rule applied to that reading), what the City schedules or closes, and what is simply unknown.

A place marker without red, yellow, or green has no current rating. Its card names the gap.

Swimming places by name

Keyboard and screen-reader alternative to the map. Colored badges appear only where a current rating exists; each card explains its evidence or gap.

Each place card names the gauge behind its color, with the reading, its time and the station’s flow history. The Hill Country water map holds each gauge’s full record wherever USGS keeps a daily one (20 of the 24).

City pool index · separate from creek flow

All City of Austin pools

The City’s index gives each pool’s and splash pad’s general status, and every one appears on the map. Closed ones are red. Open ones are colored only where their posted hours are encoded and re-checked; the rest stay uncolored with a link to their City page. A marker with a dashed outline sits at the street address, not on the pool itself. An unexpected maintenance or weather closure may not appear here yet.
